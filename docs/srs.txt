Introduction

Charitable giving and social support programs play a significant role in improving the
quality of life of vulnerable individuals and promoting community development. Governments, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), companies, and individual donors
frequently contribute financial resources, goods, and services to support people facing
economic hardship, disabilities, natural disasters, health challenges, and other social
problems. However, the effectiveness of these charitable activities largely depends on
the availability of a transparent, accountable, and well-organized management system.
In many communities, traditional charity management is still carried out using manual
or semi-digital methods. These approaches often lead to challenges such as poor record
management, lack of transparency in donation distribution, delays in verifying beneficiaries, duplicate support, limited communication between donors and administrators,
and difficulty generating accurate reports. As the number of donations and beneficiaries
increases, managing charity operations efficiently becomes increasingly difficult.
To address these challenges, this project proposes We Donate: Adama Community
Support and Charity Management System, a web-based platform designed to digitize and streamline the entire charity management process within Adama City Administration. The system provides a centralized platform that enables donors to contribute
financial assistance, physical items, and community services while allowing government
administrators to verify beneficiaries, review support requests, manage donation distribution, and monitor the overall donation process.
Unlike conventional public charity platforms, the proposed system emphasizes fairness
and accountability by ensuring that beneficiaries are identified and verified through the
appropriate government administrative structures before receiving assistance. Every donation transaction, approval process, and distribution activity is recorded within the system to improve transparency, strengthen public trust, and ensure that support reaches
those who genuinely need it.
Furthermore, the system incorporates secure user authentication, role-based access control, donation tracking, request management, reporting, notifications, and dashboard analytics to improve operational efficiency and decision-making. By replacing manual procedures with a modern digital solution, the system supports the digital transformation
initiatives of Adama City Administration while enhancing the effectiveness, reliability,
and sustainability of community support programs.

1.1

Existing System

Currently, charity and community support activities in many government offices and local
administrative institutions are managed using manual procedures. Information about
beneficiaries, donations, and support requests is typically recorded on paper documents,
notebooks, spreadsheets, or separate files maintained by different administrative offices.
The absence of a centralized digital platform makes it difficult to effectively manage,
monitor, and track charitable activities.
When individuals or families require assistance, their information is usually collected
manually by local administrative bodies such as Kebele offices. Verification processes,
beneficiary records, and support requests are handled through paper-based documentation and physical communication between different administrative levels. Similarly,
donors who wish to contribute financial support, physical items, or services often rely on
direct communication with administrative offices or community representatives.
The process of matching donations with beneficiaries is also performed manually, requiring significant time and effort from administrators. Tracking the status of donations,
confirming deliveries, generating reports, and maintaining historical records are often
challenging due to the lack of an integrated information management system. As a result, obtaining accurate and up-to-date information regarding donations and beneficiaries
becomes difficult.
Furthermore, communication among donors, beneficiaries, and administrative offices is
largely dependent on face-to-face interactions, telephone calls, and paper-based reports.
These traditional methods increase administrative workload and limit the efficiency,
transparency, and accountability of charity management activities.
Therefore, there is a need for a modern computerized system that can automate donation
management processes, improve transparency, maintain accurate records, and facilitate
efficient coordination among all stakeholders involved in community support and charity
services.
1.2

Limitations of Existing System

The existing manual charity management system has several limitations that affect the
efficiency, transparency, and reliability of community support activities. Since most processes are handled using paper-based records, spreadsheets, and physical communication,
managing a large number of donors, beneficiaries, and donation activities becomes difficult. The major limitations of the existing system are described below.

6

1.2.1

Lack of Transparency in Donation Management

In the existing system, donors have limited ability to track how their contributions are
used after donation. Information about donation collection, allocation, and distribution
is usually maintained manually, making it difficult to provide clear evidence of where and
when donations are delivered. This reduces donor confidence and creates challenges in
maintaining accountability.
1.2.2

Difficulty in Beneficiary Verification

The current system relies heavily on manual verification processes conducted by local
administrative offices. Collecting beneficiary information, checking eligibility, and maintaining updated records require significant time and effort. Due to the absence of a
centralized database, identifying genuine beneficiaries and avoiding incorrect or duplicate
registrations becomes challenging.
1.2.3

Poor Donation Tracking and Monitoring

The existing approach does not provide an effective mechanism to monitor the status of
donations from the time they are submitted until they reach beneficiaries. Administrators
may face difficulties tracking pending donations, completed distributions, and delivery
confirmations, which can result in delays and inefficient resource allocation.
1.2.4

Manual Record Management

Most charity-related information is stored in physical documents, notebooks, or separate
files. Managing and updating these records requires considerable administrative effort.
Manual record keeping also increases the possibility of data loss, duplication, inconsistency, and human errors.
1.2.5

Limited Reporting and Data Analysis

Generating reports about donations, beneficiaries, distribution activities, and community
needs is difficult in the existing system. Administrators need to manually collect and
organize information from different sources, which consumes time and may result in
inaccurate reports. The lack of analytical information also affects decision-making and
planning.
1.2.6

Lack of Centralized Communication

Communication between donors, beneficiaries, NGOs, and administrative offices is mainly
performed through direct meetings, phone calls, or informal channels. The absence of

7

a centralized communication platform makes it difficult to share updates, notifications,
and important information efficiently.
1.2.7

Increased Risk of Fraud and Duplicate Support

Since beneficiary information and donation records are not managed through an integrated system, there is a risk of fraudulent requests, repeated assistance to the same
beneficiaries, and improper allocation of resources. The lack of digital verification and
audit mechanisms reduces control over charity activities.
1.2.8

High Administrative Workload

Manual processes require administrators to spend significant time collecting data, verifying beneficiaries, managing requests, and preparing reports. This increases operational
costs and reduces the efficiency of government offices responsible for coordinating community support programs.
1.2.9

Limited Accessibility and Convenience

Donors and stakeholders cannot easily access updated information about available support requests, donation opportunities, or the progress of charity activities. The dependence on physical presence and manual communication limits participation and reduces
the overall reach of charity services.
1.3

Objectives of Proposed System

The proposed We Donate: Adama Community Support and Charity Management System aims to overcome the limitations of the existing manual charity management approach by providing a centralized, transparent, and efficient digital platform for
managing community support activities. The system focuses on improving donation management, beneficiary verification, communication, reporting, and accountability among
donors, beneficiaries, and administrative offices.
1.3.1

General Objectives

The general objective of this project is to develop a web-based charity management system
that digitizes and improves the process of donation collection, beneficiary verification,
distribution management, and monitoring within Adama City Administration.
The proposed system aims to create a reliable platform that connects donors with verified beneficiaries through government administrative structures, ensuring transparency,
fairness, and accountability in community support activities.

1.3.2

Specific Objectives

The specific objectives of the proposed system are:
• To develop an online platform for managing different types of donations, including
financial donations, physical items, and community services.
• To provide a centralized database for storing and managing information about
donors, beneficiaries, donations, requests, and distribution activities.
• To enable government administrative offices to register, verify, and approve beneficiaries before they receive community support.
• To improve transparency by allowing donation activities to be tracked from submission to final delivery.
• To reduce fraudulent requests and duplicate support through proper beneficiary
verification and record management.
• To automate charity management processes that are currently performed manually,
reducing administrative workload and improving efficiency.
• To provide role-based access control for different users, including donors, administrators, and other stakeholders, to ensure secure system usage.
• To generate accurate reports and analytical information to support decision-making
and planning by administrators.
• To improve communication between donors, beneficiaries, and administrative offices
through notifications and system updates.
• To maintain accountability by recording important activities, approvals, and transactions with responsible users and timestamps.
• To support the digital transformation initiatives of Adama City Administration by
introducing a modern and sustainable charity management solution.

Methodology and Tools

2.1

Methodology

The development of the We Donate: Adama Community Support and Charity Management System follows an Agile Software Development Methodology.
Agile is an iterative and incremental approach that allows the system to be developed,
tested, and improved through multiple development cycles.
The Agile methodology is selected because the proposed system consists of different modules, including user authentication, user management, donation management, beneficiary
verification, request management, reporting, and dashboard analytics. Developing the
system in small iterations allows the development team to implement, test, and refine
each module based on requirements and feedback.
The major activities followed during the development process include:
Requirement Analysis During this phase, the requirements of the system are collected and analyzed by identifying the problems of the existing charity management
system and defining the needs of different stakeholders, including donors, beneficiaries,
and administrative users.
System Design In this phase, the overall architecture, database structure, user interfaces, and system workflows are designed. The design focuses on creating a secure and
user-friendly platform that supports efficient charity management.
Implementation The system is developed by implementing different modules incrementally. Each module is developed, integrated, and tested before moving to the next
development stage. The main implemented modules include authentication, donation
management, beneficiary management, administration, reporting, and dashboards.
Testing Testing is performed throughout the development process to ensure that each
system component functions correctly. Different testing approaches, including unit testing, integration testing, system testing, and user acceptance testing, are considered to
verify system quality and reliability.
Deployment and Maintenance After successful testing, the system is prepared for
deployment. Continuous maintenance and improvements are performed based on user
feedback and future requirements.

System Requirement Specification

A System Requirement Specification (SRS) is a document that describes the complete requirements of a software system, including its expected functionalities, constraints,
and quality attributes. It serves as a communication document between stakeholders, developers, and users by defining what the system should accomplish and how it should
behave.
This chapter presents the system requirements for the We Donate: Adama Community Support and Charity Management System. The requirements are identified
based on the analysis of the existing charity management process and the objectives of
the proposed system.
The purpose of this chapter is to define the major requirements needed for designing and
developing the proposed system. It includes an overview of the system, feasibility study,
functional requirements, and non-functional requirements.
The We Donate system is a web-based charity management platform designed to improve
the transparency, efficiency, and accountability of community support activities within
Adama City Administration. The system provides different functionalities for donors,
beneficiaries, administrative offices, NGOs, and system administrators.
The system requirements focus on automating donation management, beneficiary verification, request processing, distribution tracking, reporting, and secure information management. By implementing these requirements, the system aims to provide a reliable
digital platform that ensures donations are properly managed and delivered to verified
beneficiaries.
3.1

Overview

The We Donate: Adama Community Support and Charity Management System is a web-based platform developed to improve the management of charity activities and community support services within Adama City Administration. The system
provides a centralized solution for managing donors, beneficiaries, donations, requests,
verification processes, and distribution activities.
The main purpose of the system is to replace traditional manual charity management processes with a digital platform that improves transparency, efficiency, and accountability.
The system enables donors to contribute financial donations, physical items, and services while ensuring that support is provided to verified beneficiaries through authorized
administrative structures.
The system consists of different modules that support the activities of various stakehold-

15

ers. Each user type has specific roles and permissions based on their responsibilities.
The major system users include:
Donor Donors are individuals, companies, NGOs, diaspora members, or organizations that provide support to the community. The system allows donors to create accounts, manage profiles, view available support requests, make donations, track donation
progress, and access donation history and receipts.
Beneficiary Beneficiaries are individuals or groups who require community support,
such as vulnerable families, disabled individuals, elderly people, students, or disasteraffected people. Beneficiary information is verified by authorized administrators before
receiving support. The system allows beneficiaries to submit requests, upload supporting
documents, and monitor request status.
Administrative Users Administrative users are responsible for managing and supervising charity activities. The system supports different administrative levels, including:
• Kebele Administrators: Responsible for registering beneficiaries, collecting information, verifying households, and confirming local delivery activities.
• Woreda Administrators: Responsible for monitoring kebele activities, reviewing
requests, approving distributions, and managing reports.
• City Administrators: Responsible for overall system management, user management, donation monitoring, reporting, auditing activities, and configuring system
settings.
NGO / Organization Users NGOs and organizations can participate in community
support activities by providing donations, services, or resources. They can manage their
organizational information and participate in approved charity programs.
System Administrator The system administrator manages the technical operation of
the platform, including user accounts, system security, access control, and configuration
management.
The major functions provided by the system include:
• Secure user registration and authentication
• Role-based access control
• Beneficiary registration and verification
• Support request management

• Donation creation and tracking
• Donation distribution monitoring
• Notification and communication management
• Report generation and analytics
• Audit trail and activity monitoring
• Data management and security
The system is designed using a modern web application architecture with React.js for

System Architecture

4.1

System Overview Architecture

The We Donate: Adama Community Support and Charity Management System follows a modern three-tier architecture that separates the presentation layer, business logic layer, and data storage layer. This architectural approach ensures scalability,
maintainability, and security of the system.
4.1.1

Presentation Layer (Frontend)

The presentation layer is built using React.js, a JavaScript library for building user interfaces. This layer is responsible for rendering the user interface, handling user interactions,
and communicating with the backend services. The frontend application is designed to
be responsive and accessible across various devices including desktop computers, tablets,
and smartphones.
Key components of the presentation layer include:
• User authentication and registration interfaces
• Role-based dashboards (Donor, Beneficiary, Administrator, NGO)
• Donation management interfaces
• Request management interfaces
• Reporting and analytics dashboards
• Notification management interfaces
4.1.2

Business Logic Layer (Backend)

The business logic layer is implemented using Node.js with Express.js framework. This
layer handles all server-side operations, business rules, and data processing. It provides
RESTful APIs that the frontend application consumes.
Key functions of the business logic layer include:
• User authentication and authorization
• Donation processing and management
• Beneficiary verification and management
• Request handling and approval workflows
• Distribution tracking and management

30

• Report generation
• Notification management
• System administration functions
4.1.3

Data Storage Layer

The data storage layer uses PostgreSQL as the primary database management system.
Prisma ORM is used to interact with the database, providing type-safe database operations and schema management.
The database stores the following major entities:
• Users (Donors, Beneficiaries, Administrators, NGOs)
• Beneficiary profiles
• Donation records
• Support requests
• Distribution records
• Notifications
• Audit logs
• System configurations

Donor Workflow

Security Considerations

6.1

Authentication and Authorization

The system implements secure authentication using JSON Web Tokens (JWT). Users
must provide valid credentials (email and password) to access the system. Passwords are
securely hashed using bcrypt before storage.

33

Role-based access control (RBAC) ensures that users can only access features and data
appropriate to their role (Donor, Beneficiary, Administrator, NGO, or System Administrator).
6.2

Data Security

All sensitive data is encrypted both in transit (using HTTPS) and at rest. User passwords are never stored in plain text. Personal information of beneficiaries and donors is
protected from unauthorized access.
6.3

Session Management

User sessions are managed securely with appropriate timeout periods. Users are automatically logged out after periods of inactivity to prevent unauthorized access.
